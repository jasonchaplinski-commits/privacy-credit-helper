Credit

1 · Get your free reports

All three bureaus give free weekly reports at AnnualCreditReport.com (the only official free site). Download each as a PDF and keep them in a private folder.

What to look for
  • Names, addresses or employers that aren't yours
  • Accounts you never opened (possible identity theft: also file at IdentityTheft.gov)
  • Late payments you didn't make, or wrong dates
  • Wrong balances or credit limits (a missing limit can hurt your score)
  • Closed accounts showing open, or paid accounts showing a balance
  • The same debt listed twice (original lender and collector both showing a balance)
  • Negative items older than about 7 years from the first missed payment
  • Hard inquiries you didn't authorize
Denied for a card or loan?

The lender must send an "adverse action" notice listing the main reasons and which bureau it used. If you didn't get one, or it's vague, use Ask a lender why I was denied below. Common causes when credit is good: a credit freeze on the bureau they pulled, too many recent applications or new accounts, no existing relationship with that bank, or income/identity checks.

Credit freezes (free)

A freeze blocks new accounts in your name. Lift it temporarily before you apply for credit, then re-freeze. Equifax · Experian · TransUnion

Stop prescreened credit card offers: OptOutPrescreen.com.

Collections & settlements

Know your rights first

  • Make them prove it. Within 30 days of a collector's first notice, send a validation request. They must pause collection until they respond with proof.
  • Check the age. Each state limits how long a creditor can sue. In some states a payment, or even agreeing you owe it, can restart that clock, so look into an old debt before paying or promising anything.
  • Get the deal in writing before you pay. The agreement should name the exact amount, say it settles the debt in full, and say how it will be reported.
  • Never give a collector ongoing access to your bank account. Pay once, by cashier's check or a one-time payment, and keep proof.
  • Don't ignore a lawsuit. If you're served court papers, respond by the deadline or you can lose automatically (then wages or bank accounts can be garnished). Contact legal aid or a lawyer.

Traffic tickets

Never ignore a ticket you were handed or formally served. Missing the court or "appear by" date usually means you're found responsible automatically, plus a license suspension and extra fees.

Add a ticket

Getting ready for a hearing
  • Go back to the spot. Take photos of speed-limit signs (or missing / hidden signs), the intersection, lights and sight lines.
  • Save dashcam video, phone GPS history or a receipt that shows where you were and when.
  • Ask for the evidence early (use the Evidence request letter): officer notes, radar or laser calibration and training records, and any dashcam or bodycam video.
  • Write down what happened while it's fresh: weather, traffic, what the officer said.
  • At the hearing, be calm and brief. Ask the officer questions about how they measured your speed and when the device was last checked.
Can a ticket be removed from my record?

Usually not once you've been found responsible, so the best time to act is before the court date: traffic school, a hearing, or a request to reduce the charge can keep it off your record. After that, points stop counting toward suspension over time (in Arizona, suspensions are based on points in the last 12 months), and insurers usually look back about 3 to 5 years. If something on your driving record is wrong, ask the court and your state's motor vehicle department to correct it.

About ticket apps: some connect you to a real lawyer for a fee; others just write a letter for you. "Robot lawyer" apps have been fined by the FTC for overpromising. For criminal charges (DUI, reckless driving, very high speeds, driving on a suspended license) talk to a lawyer.

Stop spam calls, texts & email

No single step stops all spam, but these together cut it way down. Check each one off as you do it. Removing your listings on the Remove my info tab also helps, because spammers buy phone numbers and emails from those sites.

Golden rules

  • Don't answer unknown numbers. Let them go to voicemail; real callers leave a message.
  • If you do answer a robocall, hang up. Don't press a number to "be removed" and don't say "yes"; it just confirms your number works.
  • Never click links in surprise texts or emails, even ones that look like your bank, a delivery or the IRS. Go to the company's site or app yourself.
  • Don't click "unsubscribe" in an email you think is a scam. Only unsubscribe from companies you actually signed up with.
  • Nobody legitimate asks you to pay with gift cards, crypto or wire transfers.
